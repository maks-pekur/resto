<domain>
## Phase Boundary

Give a location a way to **stop taking orders** — a one-tap operator pause from the order feed and a mandatory weekly opening schedule with date exceptions — enforced **server-side at order creation** so a restaurant cannot accept paid orders while closed or overwhelmed.

**This phase delivers:**

1. **Weekly opening schedule per location** — mandatory, up to two intervals per weekday, closed days, closes may cross midnight.
2. **Date-specific exceptions** — one-off overrides for a single date (different hours or closed), layered over the weekly schedule.
3. **One-tap pause from the order feed** — 20 min / 40 min / until next opening / closed until a date, with auto-expiry and early resume.
4. **Server-side enforcement at `CreateOrderService`** — the single chokepoint; a forged or stale client cannot create an order at a paused or closed location.
5. **A location edit path that does not exist today** — `PATCH` endpoint + aggregate mutator + admin edit form (name, address, contacts, timezone, schedule, pre-order lead time).
6. **`locations.timezone` becomes mandatory** — backfilled and `NOT NULL`; the schedule is meaningless without it.
7. **Guest-facing closed/paused state** — banner on the menu, cart CTA that routes to scheduling, live state on the checkout page, and a distinct rejection carrying the reopen time.
8. **Pre-order (scheduled) ordering repaired and gated by the schedule** — the ORD-12 deferral in `dto.ts:33` comes due here.

**Explicitly OUT of scope (deferred, each with a home):**

- **Guest location selection at checkout** → its own phase, sequenced with/after Phase 9 (Delivery Zones). See D-23. This phase makes the server ready for it.
- **Delivery-zone-driven location routing ("nearest point to the guest's address")** → Phase 9, MVP-2. Requires geocoding; no address exists for pickup/dine-in at all.
- **Per-fulfillment-mode pause UI** (stop delivery, keep pickup) → column reserved now (D-08), no UI.
- **Recurring annual holidays** → exceptions are one-off dates only (D-06).
- **Capacity / throttling / prep-time-delay-without-stopping-orders / shift systems** → outside "stop taking orders" entirely; Phase 9 / MVP-2.
- **Auto-reject or auto-cancel of already-paid orders on pause** → pause never touches the existing queue (D-12).
- **Real-time (SSE) pause propagation** → Phase 18; the 5s availability TTL is the same order of magnitude.
- **`?location=all` pause/schedule WRITE path** → contradicts 08.5 D-05; read-only visibility only (D-21).

<decisions>
## Implementation Decisions

### Schedule model — three layers

- **D-01 — The schedule is MANDATORY. A location without a schedule does not accept orders.** _Founder decision, reversing the reviewer-recommended "null schedule = always open" default._ Rationale: the schedule is how the system knows whether to take orders at all; an unset schedule is an unanswered question, not an implicit "yes".
  - **Guardrail (founder-approved):** the create-location form is **pre-filled** with a default week (10:00–22:00 every day) so a location is never born non-operational. The owner adjusts. Do not ship an empty-week form.
  - Existing locations: 08.4 D-13 records there is no production location data, and `0068_stop_list_location_regrain.sql:22-24` already deleted dev rows on that basis — backfill is a one-liner.
- **D-02 — Up to two intervals per weekday.** Lunch 11:00–15:00 + dinner 18:00–23:00 is the classic independent-restaurant shape. _Recorded disagreement:_ `persona-skeptic` (cut list #5) and `persona-cto` ("over-engineering to reject") both wanted one interval per day in MVP-1; `persona-product` HIGH-5 wanted the storage shape to allow two but the UI to ship one. Founder chose two intervals in the UI. Do not silently narrow this.
- **D-03 — Closes may cross midnight.** Friday 18:00 → 02:00 must be expressible. `persona-product` HIGH-4 confirms overnight is the norm for the bar / late-night segment, not an edge case. Store as minutes-from-local-midnight where `close > 1440` means "next day" (CTO's recommended shape).
- **D-04 — `locations.timezone` becomes NOT NULL in this phase's migration.** All three reviewers raised this as BLOCK. Today it is nullable (`packages/db/src/schema/brands.ts:133`), optional in the create DTO (`locations.controller.ts:48`), optional in the admin form (`locations.tsx:38-44`), and every consumer silently falls back to UTC (`create-order.service.ts:182`, `list-orders.service.ts:146`). A 10:00–22:00 schedule evaluated in UTC for a Moscow venue closes the kitchen at 01:00 local. **Do not add a fourth `?? 'UTC'`.** The admin form gets a real timezone picker defaulting to `Intl.DateTimeFormat().resolvedOptions().timeZone`.
- **D-05 — Date-specific exceptions, with hours.** _Founder decision, changed mid-discussion (the earlier answer was "no exceptions, the pause is enough")._ On a concrete date the owner sets **different hours** or **closed**; the exception overrides the weekly schedule for that day only. Examples the founder gave: `31.12 → 12:00–17:00`, `01.01 → closed`, `08.03 → 11:00–02:00` (extended). This resolves `persona-product` HIGH-6 more completely than the pause-based workaround that HIGH-6 proposed.
- **D-06 — Exceptions are one-off dates, never recurring.** A forgotten annual rule silently closes a working day a year later. The owner re-enters holidays each year.
- **D-07 — Evaluation order at order creation: pause → date exception → weekly schedule.** Each layer overrides the one below it.

### Pause

- **D-08 — Pause durations: 20 min / 40 min / until next opening / closed until a date.**
  - **"Until next opening" is derived from the schedule**, not from local midnight. `persona-product` HIGH-4: a bar closing at 02:00 that resolves "rest of day" to midnight silently un-pauses itself mid-service. _Recorded disagreement:_ `persona-skeptic` (cut list #4) and `persona-cto` both wanted this third button cut; the founder kept it and the schedule (D-01) makes it computable.
  - **"Closed until a date"** covers the unplanned multi-day close (chef ill, refurb). It writes the same `paused_until` field with a different reason. Distinct from D-05 exceptions: **pause = unplanned, from this second; exception = planned, on a date.** Both exist because they solve different problems.
  - **`paused_modes text[] null` is reserved in the schema now, with no UI** (`persona-product` MED-1). `null` = all modes. Makes Phase 9's per-mode pause additive instead of a migration against live pause data.
- **D-09 — Pause never blocks a scheduled (pre-order) order.** Pause is a "not right now" concept. A Saturday pre-order placed during a Friday-lunch rush must go through.
- **D-10 — Auto-resume, derived at read time from `paused_until <= now()`. No cron, no scheduler, no confirmation dialog.** A pause requiring manual resume becomes a permanently-closed restaurant the first time a shift ends mid-pause. `apps/api/src/infrastructure/jobs/` gains nothing in this phase.
- **D-11 — No pause reason is collected. One tap, no dialog.** The operator is in the middle of a rush; nobody types on a greasy tablet. The guest sees neutral wording with the reopen time ("temporarily not taking orders, back at 21:20"). _Note:_ `persona-product` BLOCK-3's original spec proposed an optional `reason?`; founder cut it. The `pause_reason` field may still exist internally to distinguish "closed until a date" from a timed pause (D-08) — it is not operator free text.
- **D-12 — Pause does not touch the existing queue.** Already-paid orders are unaffected; the kitchen finishes what it took. If it genuinely cannot, the operator cancels with a refund — that mechanism already shipped in Phase 10. Nothing is auto-rejected or auto-refunded. State this explicitly so nobody builds it.

### Guest-facing behaviour

- **D-13 — Closed/paused state is visible from the menu onward, and the checkout page polls it live.**
  - **Menu:** a banner in the same slot as `delivery-pickup-banner.tsx:11` ("Closed — opens tomorrow at 11:00"). The menu itself stays fully browsable — SEO, Phase 15 content, and pre-ordering all depend on it.
  - **Cart:** the CTA is **relabelled to route to the scheduled-time path**, not disabled. The guest can build a cart while closed.
  - **Checkout:** _founder decision._ The page polls location state live (port the `apps/qr-menu/src/App.tsx:61-79` interval + `focus` + `visibilitychange` pattern; the website today fetches availability once at RSC render, `apps/website/lib/api-client.ts:38-49`). When the location pauses, the screen changes **immediately** — the guest must not fill in a card form that cannot work.
  - **`GET /v1/menu` (`s-maxage=300`) MUST NOT carry this state** — it would either serve 5-minute-stale "open" or destroy the hit rate on the one endpoint PROJECT.md requires to be fast on cold cache. State rides `GET /v1/menu/availability` (`s-maxage=5`), whose service already resolves the same default location (`get-menu-availability.service.ts:30`).
- **D-14 — Fail OPEN on the menu when state cannot be fetched.** _Founder decision._ A network blip must not close a working restaurant. `CreateOrderService` is the only real gate; the client copy is UX. _Recorded disagreement:_ `persona-skeptic` HIGH-5 wanted fail-closed; `persona-product` MED-4 proposed fail-open on the menu / fail-closed on checkout. Founder chose fail-open. With D-13's live checkout poll, the exposure is a guest reaching a submit that then rejects cleanly (D-16).
- **D-15 — Pre-orders are allowed while closed or paused, checked against the FULFILLMENT time.** _Highest-value product decision in the phase._
  - ASAP order → `now` must be inside an open interval **and** the location must not be paused.
  - Scheduled order → the **requested slot** must be inside an open interval (weekly + exceptions); the pause is ignored entirely (D-09).
  - Both branches go through the one chokepoint at `create-order.service.ts:43`.
  - **The scheduled path is broken today and this phase fixes it.** `order-time-selector.tsx:38-45` emits a bare `datetime-local` string (`2026-08-20T18:30` — no seconds, no offset); `checkout-schema.ts:7` accepts it as `z.string().min(1)`; `checkout-form.tsx:93` forwards it verbatim; `dto.ts:34` validates with `z.string().datetime()`, which requires a `Z`. **Verified by reading the chain — planner must confirm with one request before building on it.** Fix explicitly: a bare local datetime interpreted server-side would be a second timezone bug in the same phase.
  - **Horizon: 7 days.** Today `dto.ts:34-40` accepts any future datetime (2031 included).
  - **Minimum lead time is a per-location setting**, default **60 minutes** for a new location. _Founder decision — the reviewers proposed a fixed constant._ Lives on the location edit form next to the schedule.
  - **The guest picks from available slots, not a free-text time field.** Slots are derived from the schedule + exceptions + lead time. No rejection after the form is filled.
- **D-16 — The rejection needs distinct problem codes carrying the reopen time.** `checkout-form.tsx:104-107` collapses every failure into one red error with a "Try again" button that could never work here. Add `ordering.location_paused` / `ordering.location_closed` with `reopensAt` in the RFC 7807 body, plus a dedicated checkout state that offers the scheduled-time path instead of "Try again".
- **D-17 — Re-check location state before minting the payment intent.** _Founder decision._ `create-checkout-payment.service.ts:56-59` gates only on order status; `checkout-form.tsx:110-119` re-mints intents with no re-check. Window: guest passes the open check at 22:59:50 → operator pauses at 23:00:00 → guest confirms at 23:02 → paid order at a paused venue.
  - _Recorded disagreement:_ `persona-cto` MED-3 and `persona-product` MED-5 both recommended **against** this, fearing paid-but-invisible orders. **The founder's reading is correct and the objection does not apply:** the check blocks _before_ the intent is created, so the failure mode is an abandoned unpaid `created` order, not a paid invisible one. `persona-skeptic` HIGH-6 supports the check (~5 lines).
  - **An already-authorized PaymentIntent is never revoked** — that is success criterion 2 and it stays true. Only the minting of a _new_ intent is gated.

### Permissions & placement

- **D-18 — Pause / resume → `@Permissions({ order: ['update-status'] })`.** Held by `owner`, `admin`, and all three presets (`manager`, `cashier-foh`, `kitchen`). **Zero RBAC migration, zero preset re-sync.** All three reviewers flagged that gating on `location:['update']` makes the emergency brake owner-only — a verbatim regression of the bug Phase 10 D-06 was written to fix (`system-roles.ts:14,27,33`; `preset-roles.ts:13-42` — the presets have no `location` key at all). The person who can see the flood must be able to stop it.
- **D-19 — Weekly schedule + exceptions + lead-time edit → `@Permissions({ location: ['update'] })` (owner-only today).** Schedule is configuration, not operations. No RBAC change needed. Do not conflate the two gates.
- **D-20 — The locations page is the home of location state; the feed header carries a mirror control.**
  - `/{brandSlug}/locations` gets an **edit** affordance per row (name, address, contacts, timezone, weekly schedule, exceptions, lead time) plus a per-row state chip (`Accepting` / `Paused until 21:20` / `Closed · opens 11:00`) with the pause menu on the row. This is where a multi-location owner sees and manages everything, including in `?location=all`.
  - The **orders feed header** carries a mirror state chip + one-tap pause **when a single location is selected**. This satisfies SCHED-01's "one tap from the order-feed header" without teaching the feed to render in `all` mode.
- **D-21 — No `?location=all` WRITE path.** Setting pause or schedule requires a concrete location. Consistent with 08.5 D-05 (stop-list writes stay location-scoped, read-only in `all`). Visibility in `all` comes from the locations page (D-20), which is `@LocationNeutral` already (`locations.controller.ts:86`).
- **D-22 — Authorize the location path param explicitly. Do not defer `LocationPermissionChecker` a fourth time silently.** `LocationScopeGuard` (`location-scope.guard.ts:39-85`) keys everything on the ALS-bound location from the `x-location-id` header and never on a route parameter; `@LocationNeutral()` bypasses it entirely. With `POST /v1/tenancy/locations/:id/pause` on a `@LocationNeutral` controller (required by D-21 so an owner in `all` is not 403'd), **a `cashier-foh` scoped to Location B could pause Location A by changing a UUID** — a cross-location write on the revenue path. Minimum correct fix: in the service, assert the target location is in the caller's scope via the existing `MemberLocationScopeReader` port, owner bypassing per 08.4 D-05, out-of-scope → existence-hiding 404 (~15 lines). If the planner instead wires `LocationPermissionChecker` properly, that is better — but a fourth silent deferral is not acceptable.

### Multi-location reality (founder-acknowledged limitation)

- **D-23 — Guest location selection stays out of this phase; the server is built ready for it.** The founder's model — "the site should figure out which location, and let the guest pick when it can't" — is right and is a separate phase.
  - **Today:** the guest never addresses a location. `create-order.service.ts:47` → `default-location-resolver.service.ts:16-19` returns the **oldest active** location; the same resolver backs guest availability and pricing. Admin, meanwhile, defaults the owner to the **alphabetically first** location (`use-effective-location.ts:38-40`, 08.5 D-03). For a two-location brand these are different locations, so an owner can pause the location guests are _not_ ordering into and the button visibly does nothing (`persona-skeptic` BLOCK-2).
  - **This phase:** single-location brands work correctly and completely. For multi-location brands the enforcement is honest but coarse — it applies to the resolved location.
  - **Build for the next phase now:** the open/closed check keys on **the location the order actually resolves to**; the ports and public endpoints take the location **explicitly** rather than re-deriving "the first active one" internally. The follow-on phase should then be almost entirely screens.
  - Why it is not a small addition: the stop-list is per-location (08.4 D-02), so a guest who picks a location after building a cart needs the cart re-validated against that location's stop-list; and for delivery, "which location serves this address" is a zone question (Phase 9, MVP-2).
- **D-24 — Show which location receives site orders.** On the locations page, mark the location the site currently resolves to, so the owner is never surprised by a pause that "does nothing". Cheap, honest, and removes the support ticket that looks like a bug in the button.

### Claude's Discretion (research / planner)

- **Storage shape.** All three reviewers converge on **columns on `locations`, no new table**: `orders_paused_until timestamptz null`, `pause_reason text null`, `paused_modes text[] null`, `ordering_schedule jsonb null`, `preorder_lead_minutes int`. Rationale (CTO HIGH-4, Skeptic MED-1): a child table mechanically obliges a composite FK, a RESTRICTIVE RLS migration mirroring `0069`, a **~140-line hand-authored `tenancy_erase_tenant` rewrite** (`0072`, `0074`, `0077` exist only because that was forgotten three times — and a RESTRICT FK not deleted first makes tenant erasure fail hard), and a `tenant-isolation.spec.ts` fixture — for data that is never joined, never queried across locations, never indexed. The `jsonb` shape must already allow an array of intervals per day (D-02) and a list of date exceptions (D-05).
- **ETag correctness — the most likely way this ships green and broken.** `public-menu.controller.ts:160-167` keys the availability ETag on `stopVersion`, bumped only by a stop-list write (`catalog-drizzle.repository.ts:828-840`). A schedule boundary at 21:00 produces **no write at all**, so `If-None-Match` matches and the endpoint answers `304` forever while clients render "open". Fix by deriving the ETag from the response body (e.g. `<stopVersion>.<openState>.<pausedUntilEpoch>`) or adding a coarse time bucket. **Do not bump `catalog_location_stop_version` from a tenancy write path** — that counter belongs to catalog (`packages/db/src/schema/menu.ts:414`) and reaching into it inverts the context boundary.
- **Time evaluation in TypeScript as a pure domain policy**, e.g. `ordering/domain/ordering-window.policy.ts` exporting `evaluate(state, at) → { ok } | { ok: false, reason: 'paused'|'closed', reopensAt }`, called from `CreateOrderService` after the location resolves and **before** pricing. Not in `Order.create()` — location hours are not an order invariant, and a pure function is the only way DST and midnight-crossing get real unit tests. Note: **no date library exists in this repo at all** (no `date-fns`/`luxon`/`dayjs`/`temporal` in any `package.json`), and the one hand-rolled helper `zonedMidnightUtc` (`list-orders.service.ts:163-190`) samples the offset at _UTC_ midnight and special-cases `hour === 24` — do not reuse or hand-roll a third variant without a decision.
- **Context ownership:** `tenancy` owns the write path and the columns; `ordering` reads through a Symbol port (`OrderingModule` already imports `TenancyModule`, `ordering.module.ts:27`). Do not replicate the existing concrete cross-context import at `create-order.service.ts:11`.
- **Fold the location read into the existing query.** `defaultLocation.resolveForBrand` already selects full `locations` rows and discards all but `id`; `resolveBusinessDate` re-selects the same row for `timezone` (`create-order.service.ts:178-183`). Returning `{ id, timezone, ordersPausedUntil, pausedModes, schedule, preorderLeadMinutes }` in one query makes the phase **net -1 round-trip**. Do not invent a cache for it.
- **Emit `tenancy.location_ordering_paused.v1` / `..._resumed.v1`** via `buildEnvelope` + outbox in the same transaction as the column write. The audit subscriber already wildcards `tenancy.>` (`nats-audit-subscriber.ts:20`), so "who stopped the money" becomes an `audit_log` row for free, and Phase 18 SSE gets a ready-made push signal. Needs one line in `ACTION_TARGET_KIND` and a `location` branch in the `targetId` switch (`record-audit.service.ts:7-37,76-104`). Do **not** emit an event for schedule edits (configuration, not operations). _Note:_ `ArchiveLocationService.execute` (`archive-location.service.ts:26-30`) currently drops its `LocationArchived` event on the floor by never calling `pullEvents()` — fix while in there.
- **Do not add a fourth poller to the orders page.** `orders.tsx:119-152` already runs `feedQuery` (5s), `refundFailedCountQuery` (5s), and a conditional activation check. Fold pause state into the existing feed response.
- **Validate IANA timezones for real.** `location.aggregate.ts:12-14` uses `/^[A-Za-z_]+\/[A-Za-z_]+$/` with a comment admitting no real validation — `Foo/Bar` passes and then throws a raw `Error` inside `Intl.DateTimeFormat` at order time (`create-order.service.ts:193-197`), i.e. a **500 on the guest checkout path**. `Intl.supportedValuesOf('timeZone')` is one line on Node 22.
- **Type `locations.contacts`.** It is untyped `jsonb` (`brands.ts:134`) with an ad-hoc `{phone, email}` written by the admin (`locations.tsx:86-92`). If D-20 adds an edit form, type it in the same pass rather than deferring the same field twice.
- **`showLocationBadge = false`** is hardcoded dead and threaded through `orders.tsx:155,250,257,264` — make it live under D-20 or delete it.
- **Publishing opening hours on the guest site** (`apps/website/app/contact/`) — near-zero cost once the data exists, and "what time do you open" is the most common inbound call. Planner's call whether it fits.
- **Pre-order slot generation** — where slots are computed (server endpoint vs derived client-side from the schedule payload) and their granularity.

### Test fidelity — non-negotiable for this phase

Same trap as Phase 8 and Phase 10. `apps/api/test/unit/create-order.service.spec.ts:4-19` mocks the repository, the pricing port, the sequence port **and** `DefaultLocationResolverService` — a pause check added to `CreateOrderService` can be asserted green by a spec that never read a `locations` row. And `apps/api/test/integration/create-order-idempotency.spec.ts:20` uses `const suite = dockerOk ? describe : describe.skip` (59 test files reference `dockerOk`), so the integration net silently skips without Docker.

**Required acceptance evidence:**

1. A **real-DB** test that writes a pause / a closed schedule, calls `POST /v1/orders`, and asserts a 4xx **and zero new `orders` rows read back from the database**.
2. A real-DB test that a **scheduled** order for an open future slot succeeds while the location is paused (D-09/D-15).
3. Unit tests on the window policy for: a close crossing midnight, a DST transition day, a date exception overriding the weekly schedule, and an exception on a day the weekly schedule marks closed.
4. A real-browser smoke: operator taps pause in the feed → guest's open checkout tab changes state (D-13).

## Specific Ideas

- **Founder framing on the schedule:** "график работы обязателен — по нему и будем понимать, как принимать заказы." The schedule is not a convenience setting; it is the system's answer to "should we take this order at all." Everything else (pause, exceptions) is a modifier on top of it.
- **The three-layer model the founder asked for, in their words:** the owner sets normal working hours, and can change them on specific dates — shorten for a holiday, extend for an occasion. Pause sits above both as the emergency brake for right now.
- **Pause vs "closed until a date" is a deliberate distinction:** pause is unplanned and starts this second; an exception is planned and set in advance. Do not merge them into one control.
- **One tap, no dialog, no typing.** The pause happens during a rush. Any field on that path is a field nobody fills.
- **The guest must never fill a form that cannot work.** This is why the checkout polls live (D-13) and why the guest picks from real slots rather than a free datetime field (D-15).
- **Reviewers' premise correction, accepted by the founder:** the pause does not solve "a tenant takes paid orders at 04:00" — at 04:00 nobody is holding the tablet. Only the schedule does. If this phase has to be cut, cut the pause, not the schedule.

## Deferred Ideas

- **Guest location selection at checkout** — the guest picks which point to collect from / order into; the cart is then re-validated against that location's stop-list. Its own phase, sequenced with or after Phase 9 (Delivery Zones), because delivery routing needs zones and geocoding. This phase makes the server ready by keying everything on an explicit location (D-23).
  - **Founder's described flow (2026-08-18), captured verbatim in intent:** a modal with two tabs — **Pickup** (choose a collection point) and **Delivery** (enter an address, resolve the point from it). The founder additionally proposed gating add-to-cart behind guest sign-in and requesting browser geolocation for new guests to auto-assign a location.
  - **Claude's recorded counter-recommendation, accepted by the founder for now:** trigger the modal at **checkout**, not at add-to-cart, and do **not** require sign-in. Rationale: a login wall before the cart is the highest-yield way to lose food orders (Wolt / Uber Eats / Toast / Square all build a cart anonymously and ask for identity last); browser geolocation is refused by a majority of users and is accurate to roughly a city block — it cannot pick between two branches on nearby streets, and delivery needs a full address regardless.

A guest account belongs with **Phase 12 (CRM, MVP-2)**, where order history and loyalty make it worth the friction.
  - Rough shape of the follow-on work, in dependency order: (1) location-picker modal at checkout for pickup/dine-in + cart re-validation against the chosen location's stop-list; (2) address → location resolution, which is Phase 9 (zones + geocoding); (3) guest accounts, with Phase 12.
