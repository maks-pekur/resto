# Phase 08.3 — Skeptic Review (Owner-managed Roles & Permissions)

**Reviewer:** persona-skeptic
**Date:** 2026-07-04
**Verdict tag:** PREMATURE + UNDER-SPECIFIED (build a 20% slice, not the full role-builder)

---

## Verdict (one paragraph)

This phase is **premature for the MVP-1 first-paying-customer gate and simultaneously
under-specified on the one hard problem it exists to solve.** The founder's own scoping
already told the truth twice: `PROJECT.md:145` lists "Full RBAC customization in v1 —
per-tenant custom roles defer to post-MVP" as explicit Out-of-Scope, and Phase 17
(the team-management surface these roles would be assigned through) is **trigger-gated on
"first paying tenant adds a 2nd member with role ≠ owner"** (`ROADMAP.md:41`, `:592`) and
is NOT active. The first paying customer per `PROJECT.md:155-160` is a 1–10-location
independent restaurant; realistically the first one is single-brand, sole-owner, maybe 2
staff — a population for which the three fixed roles (`owner`/`admin`/`staff`) and the
existing invite form (`apps/admin/src/components/team/invite-form.tsx`) already suffice.
Meanwhile the actual hard deliverable — the creator-subset privilege-escalation guard — is
a defense against a threat (a mid-tier operator escalating their own privileges) that only
becomes reachable **once tenants have many members with a permission to create roles**,
which is exactly the population that does not exist yet. And the phase's draft success
criteria (`ROADMAP.md:543-546`) omit every landmine a prior audit already found: the `ac`
resource that makes `createRole` reachable at all, the `tenant:delete`/`tenant:transfer`
exclusions, role-membership lifecycle (delete/edit/orphan/lockout), the `:`-in-action-name
serialization bug, the custom-role ↔ brand-scope orthogonality gap, and the deferred
BA 1.4→1.6 CVE migration that sits underneath the very plugin being switched on. Build the
minimal version — 2–3 preset role templates the owner can lightly tweak, gated behind the
same trigger as Phase 17 — or defer the whole thing behind Phase 10 (the revenue spine).

## Findings

### CRITICAL-1 — The phase turns on a plugin that a prior audit found is DEAD without unshipped groundwork, and the SC don't mention it

**Assumption:** "Turn on `dynamicAccessControl` and reuse existing primitives" (`ROADMAP.md:536`)
implies flipping `auth.config.ts:212` is most of the work.

**Reality:** `AUDIT.md:128` already investigated this exact surface and recorded:

> `createRole` требует `ac:['create']`, которого нет ни у одной роли RestOS — dynamic AC
> фактически мёртв.

There is **no `ac` resource in `PERMISSIONS_STATEMENT`** (`packages/domain/src/rbac/permissions.ts:10-19`
— resources are `menu/order/staff/reports/settings/billing/tenant/brand`, no `ac`). BA's
`createRole` endpoint is gated on an `ac:['create']`/`ac:['read']` permission the catalog
doesn't define and no system role grants. So "turn the flag on" is a no-op until you (a) add
an `ac` resource to `PERMISSIONS_STATEMENT`, (b) grant it to `owner` in `SYSTEM_ROLES`, (c)
re-run the RBAC sync/seed, and (d) accept that `owner` now carries a self-referential
"can-create-roles" capability. None of this is in the four draft SC (`ROADMAP.md:543-546`).

**Why it bites:** The phase will be scoped and estimated as "flip a flag + build a UI" when
the real work includes editing the immutable-by-convention permission catalog
(`packages/domain/CLAUDE.md` "System roles are immutable in code") and re-seeding BA. Worse,
`staff:role:create`/`staff:role:update` **already exist** in the catalog and are already
granted to `owner`+`admin` (`permissions.ts:13`, `system-roles.ts:14,23`) — so there is a
pre-existing, half-designed role-permission surface that nobody wired, and the phase must
reconcile "is role-creation gated on `staff:roleCreate` or on BA's `ac:create`?" before any
UI. That's a design decision, not an implementation detail.

**Recommendation (must-add if built):** Make "add + grant the `ac` resource, re-seed, and
decide `ac:create` vs `staff:roleCreate` as the single gate" an explicit SC. Do not let this
be discovered mid-plan.

### CRITICAL-2 — Custom roles and brand-scope are ORTHOGONAL; a custom role grants tenant-wide power and bypasses everything 08.2 just shipped

**Assumption:** Phase 08.2 established brand isolation; 08.3 "builds ON 08.2's access core"
(`ROADMAP.md:500`), so custom roles inherit brand scoping.

**Reality:** They do not. `BrandScopeGuard` (`brand-scope.guard.ts:57-74`) keys on exactly
two things: `principal.baseRole === 'owner'` (bypass) and the separate `member_brand_scope`
table (`:64`). It has **zero awareness of `organization_role`**. Permissions (BA
`hasPermission`, `permission-checker.adapter.ts:46`) answer "what can this member do";
brand-scope answers "in which brand" — two independent subsystems. A custom role assigned in
BA's `organization_role` (tenant-scoped, `schema/auth.ts:68-73`) grants its permissions
**tenant-wide**, across every brand, unless the member also has a matching `member_brand_scope`
row. After 08.2's default-deny flip (`PROJECT.md:61`), an empty scope grants no brand — so a
custom role could grant `menu:update` but the member still can't reach any brand's menu, or
conversely a member scoped to Brand A with a custom `menu:update` role can hit Brand A's menu
but the owner will reasonably expect the role to be the whole story.

**Why it bites:** The founder's mental model (`08.2-CONTEXT.md:185-193`) is "owner creates a
role, assigns it to staff, staff can now do those things." But "those things" has no brand
dimension in `organization_role`. The first time an owner creates a "Brand-A Manager" role and
assigns it, they will be surprised it either does nothing (no brand scope) or applies
tenant-wide (all brands). The phase draft SC (`ROADMAP.md:543-546`) never mentions the
interaction. This is the single most likely source of a "permissions don't work the way I set
them" support fire.

**Recommendation (must-add):** An explicit SC and threat-model note on how a custom role's
effective authority composes with `member_brand_scope`. Decide and document: does a custom
role imply a brand assignment, or are they set independently in the same UI? If independent,
the UI must show both or owners will mis-configure.

### CRITICAL-3 — Draft SC omit the entire role-membership lifecycle; every omission is a live-shift outage or a lockout

**Omission:** The four SC (`ROADMAP.md:543-546`) cover create-role, assign-role, creator-subset,
and coexistence-with-system-roles. They say **nothing** about mutation/deletion of a role that
has live members. The founder's own Skeptic brief (`PROJECT.md:202`) explicitly lists "MVP
omissions (returns, cook-side cancellations, post-order mutations, concurrent menu edits)" as
the thing to hunt — the role-lifecycle equivalents are:

- **Delete a role that has members on it** — orphaned assignment; does the member fall back to
  a system role, to no permissions, or does the delete block? Undefined.
- **Edit a role's permissions out from under an active session** — BA resolves permissions per
  request via `hasPermission` (`permission-checker.adapter.ts:46`), so an edit takes effect on
  the member's _next request_ mid-shift. A staff member loses `order:update-status` in the
  middle of Friday dinner service. No SC addresses session/permission-change UX.
- **Sole-owner lockout** — if role editing can touch the owner's own effective permissions
  (e.g. an owner creates a restrictive role and assigns it to themselves, or an `admin` with
  `staff:roleCreate` crafts a role and the owner is demoted), there is no "last owner cannot be
  locked out" invariant anywhere in the SC. `AUDIT.md:129` already flagged that
  `tenant:delete`/`tenant:transfer`/`staff:remove` must be excluded from tenant-assignable
  roles — the lockout family is broader than that.
- **Custom role named to shadow a system role** — `organization_role.role` is a free-text
  `text` column (`schema/auth.ts:73`); nothing stops a tenant creating a role literally named
  `owner`. Reserved-name enforcement (the analogue of 08.2's reserved-slug guard,
  `08.2-CONTEXT.md:D-06`) is not in the SC.

**Why it bites:** These are not edge cases; they are the _first week_ of any tenant that
actually uses custom roles. Shipping create+assign without delete/edit/lockout semantics is
shipping a footgun.

**Recommendation (must-add):** SC for: role-delete-with-members policy, edit-propagation
behavior (and whether to force re-login / show "permissions changed" toast), an explicit
last-owner-lockout invariant with a regression test, and reserved-role-name enforcement mirroring
the reserved-slug guard.

### HIGH-1 — This is being built ahead of any user who has non-owner staff to assign roles to

**Assumption:** The owner role-builder is "the founder's headline access feature"
(`ROADMAP.md:534`, `08.2-CONTEXT.md:185`) and therefore MVP-1.

**Reality:** The gating population does not exist. Phase 17 (member list, role-change, revoke —
the surface a role would be _managed_ through) is trigger-gated on "first paying tenant adds a
2nd member with role ≠ owner" (`ROADMAP.md:41`). SEED-001 itself says the access model "only
bites once tenants have non-owner members" (`SEED-001:31`, `:116`). Today the team surface is
invite-only: `team.tsx` renders a pending-invitations table and an invite button — no member
list, no role management. The existing `invite-form.tsx:10-14` already does inviter-subset
gating (owner→all, admin→admin/staff, member→staff) for the _three fixed roles_, which is
enough for the first customer. There is no evidence in the planning corpus of a real user with
a staffing structure that the three roles cannot express.

**Why it bites:** Every week spent on a role-builder + creator-subset guard + permission-matrix
UI is a week not spent on Phase 10 (Admin Order Intake), which `ROADMAP.md:37` marks as
kept-in-MVP-1 because "the operator must see paid orders." The revenue spine is unfinished
(Phase 7.5 prod deploy deferred, Phase 10 not started); custom RBAC is being pulled ahead of
it. Cost of deferring 08.3 behind Phase 10: **near zero** — the three fixed roles cover the
first customer, and the primitives (`PERMISSIONS_STATEMENT`, `SYSTEM_ROLES`, permission-checker)
already exist and will still exist later.

**Recommendation (defer):** Move 08.3 behind Phase 10, and gate it on the _same_ trigger as
Phase 17 (first multi-member tenant with a non-owner). If a specific early customer materializes
with a genuine "the three roles don't fit" need, revisit — but build for that user, not for a
hypothetical one.

### HIGH-2 — The creator-subset guard is a strong lock on a door nobody can reach yet

**Assumption:** The creator-subset guard "IS the gate that kept it disabled" (`ROADMAP.md:540`)
and is therefore essential.

**Reality:** It is essential _only if_ dynamic role-creation is exposed to non-owners. Today
the only principal with role-creation intent is the `owner`, who by definition already holds
every permission (`SYSTEM_ROLES.owner` = full statement, `system-roles.ts:11-20`), so the
"creator can only grant permissions they hold" subset check is **vacuously true for an owner** —
an owner holds everything, so the subset is everything. The guard only earns its complexity once
`admin`-tier (or a custom role) can create roles — i.e. once `staff:roleCreate`/`ac:create` is
granted below owner. `AUDIT.md:128` confirms the risk is "чисто латентный" (purely latent):
`createRole` is currently unreachable.

**Why it bites:** The phase is being framed as "we must build the guard before we can turn the
flag on," but if role-creation is owner-only (the MVP-minimal shape), the guard is a no-op you
can ship as a one-line assertion (`caller must be owner`) plus the `tenant:delete`/`transfer`/
`staff:remove` exclusion from `AUDIT.md:129`. The elaborate subset-diff machinery is deferred
work masquerading as a blocker. The deferral wisdom ("off until creator-subset enforced") was
correct _for a multi-tier role-creation world_; re-litigating it into "build the full guard now"
is over-correcting.

**Recommendation (minimal-version):** If built at all, scope role-creation to **owner-only** for
MVP. Then the creator-subset guard collapses to: (1) caller is owner, (2) role cannot include
`tenant:delete`/`tenant:transfer`/`staff:remove`/`ac:*` (the non-delegable set). Full
creator-subset diffing lands with Phase 08.4/17 when sub-owner role-creation is actually a need.

### HIGH-3 — Building a permission-catalog UI on top of a known serialization ambiguity (`:` in action names)

**Assumption:** Reuse `PERMISSIONS_STATEMENT` as the catalog the owner toggles from
(`ROADMAP.md:536`).

**Reality:** The catalog **violates its own domain rule.** `packages/domain/CLAUDE.md` states:

> **Action names MUST NOT contain `:`.** If permissions are ever serialised as
> `resource:action` strings … names like `staff:role:create` become ambiguous. Use
> `staff:roleCreate`.

Yet `permissions.ts:13` ships `staff: ['invite', 'remove', 'role:create', 'role:update']` and
`system-roles.ts:14,23` grant `'role:create'`, `'role:update'`. The action `role:create`
contains a `:`. The moment a UI (or BA's own `hasPermission` body serialization) flattens
`resource:action`, `staff:role:create` is ambiguous with a hypothetical `staff:role`
resource + `create` action. A permission-toggle UI is exactly the consumer that serializes
these into checkbox keys / form field names / URL params.

**Why it bites:** You are about to build a UI whose entire job is to render, key, and persist
`resource:action` pairs — directly on top of the two action names that the domain's own rules
forbid. If the UI keys checkboxes by `${resource}:${action}` (the obvious choice), `staff` gets
a checkbox keyed `staff:role:create` that no parser can unambiguously round-trip.

**Recommendation (must-fix-first):** Rename `role:create`→`roleCreate`, `role:update`→`roleUpdate`
in `permissions.ts` + `system-roles.ts` + the BA seed **before** any UI work, with a regression
test. This is a small pre-req that prevents a persistence-layer bug baked into the UI.

### HIGH-4 — Third-party dependency exposure: switching on the least-exercised BA subsystem while a BA-major CVE migration is already deferred underneath it

**Assumption:** `dynamicAccessControl` is a supported, ready primitive ("already integrated,
gated off," `08.2-CONTEXT.md:188`).

**Reality:** Three compounding risks:

1. BA is pinned `=1.4.22` exactly (`apps/api/package.json:34`) precisely because BA's org/RBAC
   surface is unstable — `PERMISSIONS.md`-adjacent `PERSONA-REVIEWS.md:47-49` called BA "a
   pre-1.0-stability surface" and made version bumps a deliberate phase deliverable.
2. A **framework-major CVE migration `better-auth 1.4→1.6` is already deferred**
   (`STATE.md:215`, `dependency-cve-deferral.md`), including a _critical_ vitest RCE that BA 1.6
   drops. So the plugin being switched on sits on a version with a known-deferred security
   migration path.
3. The org plugin is already so type-broken in 1.4.22 that the codebase casts it
   `as unknown as BetterAuthPlugin` (`auth.config.ts:206-207`) and the permission checker casts
   `auth.api as unknown` (`permission-checker.adapter.ts:36`). Turning on a _further_ org-plugin
   feature (`dynamicAccessControl`) on this cast-riddled surface expands the blast radius of any
   BA behavior change.

**Why it bites:** `dynamicAccessControl` is the least-battle-tested corner of an already-unstable
plugin. If it has a bug (permission bleed across tenants, role-resolution edge case), the blast
radius is _authorization correctness_ — the highest-severity class. And the fix path
(upgrade BA) is a deferred framework-major you've explicitly chosen not to take pre-launch.

**Recommendation (must-do):** Before enabling, spend a spike verifying `dynamicAccessControl`
in 1.4.22 specifically: does `createRole` + `hasPermission` respect `organization_role` RLS
(`migrations/0005_identity_rls.sql:32-38`)? Is there a cross-tenant role-resolution path? If the
answer requires 1.6, this phase should _wait for_ the CVE migration, not front-run it.

### MEDIUM-1 — Over-engineering smells in the "headline feature" framing

**Smells to cut for a 1-brand / 2-staff first customer:**

- **Full permission-matrix UI** (`ROADMAP.md:543` "selects its permissions from the catalog")
  — a grid of 8 resources × up-to-4 actions = ~19 toggles is a lot of surface for an owner who
  wants "let this person edit the menu but not touch payouts." Preset templates cover 90%.
- **Arbitrary role composition per member** — if the UI allows N custom roles per member, the
  effective-permission resolution (`ROADMAP.md:545` "resolves system role + custom role") becomes
  a union/precedence problem. For MVP, one role per member (system OR one custom) is enough.
- **Per-brand custom roles** — not in the draft SC, but the founder vision
  (`08.2-CONTEXT.md:191-193`) drifts toward location/brand-scoped roles. That's Phase 08.4
  territory; do not let it leak into 08.3.

**Recommendation (minimal-version):** Ship **2–3 named preset templates** ("Menu Manager",
"Order Handler", "Full Admin") that the owner can lightly tweak (toggle a handful of high-level
capabilities, not a 19-checkbox matrix), one role per member, owner-authored only. This is the
80/20 and it sidesteps composition/precedence entirely.

### MEDIUM-2 — "Reuse the permission-checker that already anticipates tenant-defined roles" is asserted, not evidenced

**Claim:** `ROADMAP.md:536` and `:498` both state the permission-checker "already anticipates
tenant-defined roles."

**Reality:** `permission-checker.adapter.ts` simply forwards to BA's `hasPermission`
(`:46-49`) and trusts BA to resolve "member's system role + any tenant-defined custom role"
(per its own docstring `:10-12`). That's not the app-side "anticipating" custom roles — it's
delegating entirely to BA. There is no app-layer code that reads `organization_role`, merges
permissions, or handles the system-role-plus-custom-role precedence. So SC#4
(`ROADMAP.md:546` "permission-checker resolves system role + custom role") is really "verify BA
does this correctly," which loops back to HIGH-4 (trusting the least-tested BA subsystem).

**Recommendation:** Reframe SC#4 as a _verification_ task with an explicit cross-tenant +
system-vs-custom precedence test matrix, not a "reuse existing" freebie.

---

### LOW-1 — `assert-system-roles-present.ts` boot guard may fight dynamic roles

**Observation:** `apps/api/src/bootstrap/assert-system-roles-present.ts` treats
`organization_role` as "BA's DYNAMIC tenant-creatable role storage" (its own comment `:2`) and
asserts the three system roles are present. Once tenants start writing custom rows to that
table, confirm the boot assertion filters to system-role slugs and doesn't drift-fail on a
tenant's custom role. Low severity, but a boot-time regression is the kind that surfaces at the
worst moment.

## If I could change 3 things about this phase's scope

1. **Defer the whole phase behind Phase 10 and gate it on the Phase-17 trigger (first paying
   tenant with a non-owner member).** The three fixed roles + existing invite form cover the
   first customer; the revenue spine (prod deploy, order intake) is unfinished. Nothing is lost
   by waiting — the primitives already exist. Do not build a role-builder for zero users while
   "the operator must see paid orders" (Phase 10) is unstarted.

2. **If it must ship, replace the full permission-matrix role-builder with 2–3 owner-tweakable
   preset templates, owner-authored only, one role per member.** This collapses the
   creator-subset guard to "caller is owner + non-delegable exclusion set
   (`tenant:delete`/`transfer`/`staff:remove`/`ac:*`)," sidesteps role-composition/precedence,
   and eliminates the permission-matrix UI on top of a `:`-ambiguous catalog.

3. **Add the missing hard-problem SC that the draft omits and a prior audit already found:**
   (a) rename `role:create`→`roleCreate` first; (b) add + grant the `ac` resource and decide the
   single role-creation gate; (c) the custom-role ↔ `member_brand_scope` composition rule;
   (d) the full role-membership lifecycle — delete-with-members, edit-propagation, last-owner
   lockout invariant, reserved-role-name enforcement; (e) a BA-1.4.22-specific
   `dynamicAccessControl` correctness/cross-tenant spike before enabling, tied to the deferred
   1.4→1.6 CVE decision.
