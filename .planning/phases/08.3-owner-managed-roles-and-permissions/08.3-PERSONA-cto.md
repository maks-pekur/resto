# Phase 08.3 — CTO Persona Review (Owner-managed Roles & Permissions)

**Reviewer lens:** CTO — technical strategy + execution leverage + blast-radius containment.
**Date:** 2026-07-04
**Verdict:** Right feature, right time (08.2's access core is the correct foundation), but the phase as drafted **underestimates how much of "the guard" Better Auth already ships — and, more dangerously, exactly where BA's native guard does NOT protect you.** The creator-subset check exists in BA 1.4.22 for role *creation*, but there is **no subset check on role *assignment***, and RestOS's custom permission statement is structurally incompatible with BA's dynamic-AC role-CRUD gate. Both must be closed before `dynamicAccessControl: { enabled: true }` ships, or this phase re-introduces the exact privilege-escalation surface it claims to gate.

The good news for velocity: this is a **build-on-BA** phase, not a build-your-own-RBAC phase. The subset engine (`hasPermissionFn`, comma-separated multi-role, DB-backed custom-role resolution) is already there and correct. The work is (a) making RestOS's statement speak BA's role-management vocabulary, (b) closing the assignment-path escalation with a `beforeUpdateMemberRole` hook, (c) an allowlist of non-delegatable permissions, (d) audit + UI. Do not rebuild the checker.

---

## Findings

### CRITICAL

| # | Concern | Why it matters | Recommendation |
|---|---------|----------------|----------------|
| **C-1** | **Role *assignment* has no creator-subset check — the escalation path the phase names but does not close.** BA's `updateMemberRole` (`crud-members.mjs:279-284`) gates ONLY on `{ member: ['update'] }` + a creator-role special case. It does **not** verify that the assigned role's permissions are a subset of the assigner's. The creator-subset guard in `checkIfMemberHasPermission` (`crud-access-control.mjs:600`) runs on role **creation/update/delete only**. | An `admin` holds `member: update`-equivalent power in RestOS (`SYSTEM_ROLES.admin` in `system-roles.ts:21`). If ANY custom role in the tenant carries `tenant:delete`/`billing:update` (e.g. an owner made a "GM" role, or admin created one within their own subset), an admin — or a staff member with an over-broad custom role that includes staff role-management — can **assign that role to themselves or a peer** and escalate. Creation-subset ≠ assignment-subset. This is the single highest-severity gap. | Wire `organizationHooks.beforeUpdateMemberRole` (exists in BA 1.4.22 — used at `crud-members.mjs:290`) and enforce: the *effective permission set of every role being assigned* MUST be a subset of the **assigner's effective permissions**, AND must not contain any non-delegatable permission (see C-3). Add a self-assignment guard: an actor may never raise their own effective permissions. Prove with an e2e: admin assigns a `tenant:delete`-bearing role to self → 403. |
| **C-2** | **RestOS's `PERMISSIONS_STATEMENT` has no `ac` resource — BA's dynamic-AC role-CRUD gate cannot authorize anyone, including the owner.** When you pass your own `ac`/`roles` into `organization({ ac, roles })` (`auth.config.ts:208-210`, `access-control.ts:4-8`), BA evaluates role-management with `permissions: { ac: ['create'|'read'|'update'|'delete'] }` (`crud-access-control.mjs:81-85`). I verified against the installed 1.4.22: `ac.newRole(SYSTEM_ROLES.owner).authorize({ ac: ['create'] })` → `{ success: false, error: "not allowed to access resource: ac" }`. RestOS's owner/admin roles override BA's defaults (`organization.mjs:140-142` `{...defaultRoles, ...opts.roles}`) and carry no `ac` key. | Turning on `dynamicAccessControl` as-is makes create-role/update-role/delete-role return **403 for everyone**, or (worse, if a future dev "fixes" it by dropping the custom `ac`) silently reverts to BA's default statement and detaches from `PERMISSIONS_STATEMENT`. The feature literally cannot function without this. RestOS's current scheme encodes role-management as `staff: ['role:create','role:update']` (`permissions.ts:13`) — a vocabulary BA's dynamic AC never consults. | Add an `ac` resource to `PERMISSIONS_STATEMENT` (`ac: ['create','read','update','delete']`) and grant it to `owner` (and, per founder decision, `admin`). Map RestOS's intent onto BA's contract rather than inventing a parallel one. Retire or reconcile `staff:role:create`/`staff:role:update` — having two role-management vocabularies is a drift trap. Regression test: owner can create a role; staff (no `ac`) gets 403. |
| **C-3** | **Pure creator-subset is insufficient for blast-radius containment — high-consequence permissions must be non-delegatable even when the owner holds them.** The owner holds `tenant:delete`, `tenant:transfer`, `billing:update` (`system-roles.ts:11-19`). Under pure creator-subset (BA's model), the owner can mint a custom role containing `tenant:transfer` and assign it. Combined with C-1, that permission can then flow to an admin/staff who was never meant to have it, and (via a custom role) survives even the owner's later intent. | `tenant:transfer` = give the whole company to another account. `tenant:delete` = destroy the tenant. `billing:update` = change who gets paid. These are owner-identity operations, not delegable job functions. A restaurant owner clicking through a role-builder does not have the threat model to reason about this; the platform must. Silo-per-tenant isolation is good, but *within* a tenant the blast radius of a fat-fingered custom role is the entire business. | Introduce a `NON_DELEGATABLE` allowlist in `@resto/domain` (e.g. `tenant:delete`, `tenant:transfer`, `billing:update`, and the `ac:*` role-management perms themselves). Reject any create/update-role or assign-role request that includes one, in BOTH the create path (`beforeCreateRole` if available, else a NestJS wrapper controller in front of BA's route) and the assign path (C-1's hook). These stay bound to the immutable `owner` system role only. Document the decision — this is a founder-grade policy call (see Top-3). |

### HIGH

| # | Concern | Why it matters | Recommendation |
|---|---------|----------------|----------------|
| **H-1** | **`AuthGuard.lookupBaseRole` collapses any custom/multi-role member to `undefined` baseRole (`auth.guard.ts:153-165`).** It only recognizes the literal strings `owner`/`admin`/`staff`; `member.role` is a **comma-separated CSV** in BA (`permission.mjs:4` `input.role.split(",")`). A staff member assigned a custom role becomes `role = "staff,gm"` → `lookupBaseRole` returns `undefined`. | `BrandScopeGuard` (`brand-scope.guard.ts:57`) branches on `principal.baseRole === 'owner'` for the owner free-switch bypass, and downstream code trusts `baseRole`. A member whose role string stops matching the three literals silently loses their base-role identity. Best case: they lose owner bypass (fail-closed, annoying). Worst case: a future `if (!baseRole) allow` somewhere fails-open. Either way, custom roles + this lookup interact badly and it's not covered by any test today. | Parse `member.role` as a CSV in `lookupBaseRole`: derive `baseRole` = the highest-privilege **system** role present in the set (owner > admin > staff), independent of custom roles. Add a unit test for `"staff,gm"` and `"admin,shift-lead"`. This is a small, contained fix but it gates correctness of every brand-scoped route once custom roles exist. |
| **H-2** | **Role-*definition* changes (create/update/delete a role and its permission set) emit NO audit event.** BA 1.4.22 ships hooks only for member-role *assignment* (`afterUpdateMemberRole`, wired at `auth.config.ts:232`). There are no `beforeCreateRole`/`afterCreateRole` hooks (verified — none in `types.d.mts`). The existing `identity.role_changed.v1` contract (`contracts/identity.ts:107`) carries `previousRole`/`newRole` **slugs**, not permission-set deltas, and `ACTION_TARGET_KIND` (`record-audit.service.ts:20`) maps `identity.role_changed → user`. | "Who granted `billing:update` to the 'GM' role, and when?" is unanswerable. For a privilege-escalation surface, the *definition* of what a role can do is exactly what an incident responder needs. GDPR/security posture (per CLAUDE.md) mandates an audit of PII/authz touches; role-permission mutation is an authz touch. | Emit new events — `identity.role_created.v1` / `identity.role_permissions_changed.v1` / `identity.role_deleted.v1` — from a thin NestJS controller/service that wraps BA's role-CRUD routes (you need that wrapper anyway for C-2/C-3 enforcement and subset checks). Payload carries the permission set (or a diff) + `actorUserId`. Extend `ACTION_TARGET_KIND` with a `role` target kind. Do NOT rely on BA hooks that don't exist. |
| **H-3** | **`maximumRolesPerOrganization` defaults to `Infinity` (`crud-access-control.mjs:12`).** No cap on custom roles per tenant. | Unbounded role creation is a per-tenant abuse/DoS vector and a permission-resolution cost multiplier: `hasPermission` in dynamic-AC mode does `adapter.findMany({ model: 'organizationRole' })` per org (`has-permission.mjs:11-24`) and rebuilds `acRoles` — under peak-Friday load with a tenant that has 10k junk roles, this is an unbounded per-request read + parse. There IS a memory cache (`cacheAllRoles`, `useMemoryCache`) but it's process-local and unbounded, with no documented invalidation on role edit. | Set `dynamicAccessControl.maximumRolesPerOrganization` to a sane cap (e.g. 25–50 for a restaurant) at `auth.config.ts`. Confirm the `cacheAllRoles` invalidation story: when a role's permissions are edited or a member is reassigned, stale cache must not let old permissions survive on the next request (SC#2 says "on the next request"). If BA's memory cache is not invalidated on role update, either disable `useMemoryCache` on the hot path or add explicit invalidation — and add a test that a permission removed from a role is denied on the very next request. |

### MEDIUM

| # | Concern | Why it matters | Recommendation |
|---|---------|----------------|----------------|
| **M-1** | **`staff:role:create` / `staff:role:update` action names contain `:` — a violation of `packages/domain/CLAUDE.md` (RBAC: "Action names MUST NOT contain `:`").** Present in `permissions.ts:13` and `system-roles.ts:14,24`. | Latent, not yet active: no code serializes permissions as flat `resource:action` today (I grepped the admin — no `split(':')`). But BA's own error path already does `${key}:${x.resource[key][0]}` (`crud-access-control.mjs:615`), yielding `staff:role:create` — ambiguous if ever parsed. The moment the 08.3 admin UI renders a permission catalogue as flat strings (a natural implementation), this becomes a real bug: `"staff:role:create".split(':')` → resource `staff`, action `role`, orphan `create`. | Resolve as part of C-2: if role-management moves to an `ac` resource, delete `staff:role:create`/`staff:role:update` entirely. If they stay for any reason, rename to `staff:roleCreate`/`staff:roleUpdate` per the CLAUDE.md rule. Add the property test the RBAC rules already require. The UI catalogue MUST render `{resource, action}` structurally, never a flat joined string. |
| **M-2** | **Custom roles are org(=tenant)-scoped, but 08.2 introduced brand-scope as an orthogonal axis — verify roles cannot widen brand reach.** BA `organization_role` is keyed by `organizationId` (= RestOS tenant). Brand access is enforced separately by `BrandScopeGuard` + `member_brand_scope` (08.2). A custom role grants WHAT actions; brand scope grants WHICH brands. | If done right, orthogonal and fine. The risk is a custom role that includes a brand-management permission (`brand:create`, `brand:read` — in `PERMISSIONS_STATEMENT`) being read as widening *scope* rather than *action*. It doesn't — `BrandScopeGuard` runs independently of the permission check — but this must be explicitly verified, because a reviewer/founder could reasonably conflate them. | Add an isolation e2e: a staff member with a custom role containing `brand:read`/`menu:update` but scoped (via `member_brand_scope`) to brand A still gets existence-hiding 404 on brand B. Assert the two axes compose (deny if EITHER fails). Document the orthogonality in the CONTEXT so 08.4 (locations) builds on it correctly. |
| **M-3** | **Cross-tenant role bleed via `organizationId` in request body.** BA's create/update/delete/assign routes accept an optional `organizationId` in the body and fall back to the session's active org (`crud-access-control.mjs:52`, `crud-members.mjs:248`). BA re-checks membership in that org before acting. | This is BA's correct behavior, but RestOS layers `TenantContextMiddleware` + `auth.tenant_mismatch` cross-check (`auth.guard.ts:112-123`) on top, keyed on the *request* tenant. If the 08.3 admin calls BA's role endpoints directly (client SDK) rather than through a RestOS-wrapped controller, the RestOS tenant cross-check may not run on that call path — you'd be relying solely on BA's membership check. | Route ALL role CRUD + assignment through RestOS controllers (which you need anyway for C-1/C-2/C-3/H-2), never expose BA's `/api/auth/organization/*-role` endpoints directly to the browser. In those controllers, pin `organizationId` from the server-resolved tenant context, never from the client body. Add a cross-tenant e2e: authed as tenant A, attempt role op with `organizationId: B` → existence-hiding 404. |
| **M-4** | **No rate-limit posture stated for role-management endpoints.** RestOS has `RateLimitGuard` (per CLAUDE.md shared middleware) and a per-tenant signin rate-limit (Phase 3 D-20). Role CRUD + assignment are unmentioned. | Privilege-mutation endpoints are exactly where you want throttling — both to bound the C-3/H-3 abuse surface and to make brute-force enumeration of the subset-check boundary expensive. | Apply `RateLimitGuard` to the role-management controllers with a conservative per-tenant budget. Cheap to add given the guard exists. |

## Top 3 decisions the founder must make

1. **Non-delegatable permission policy (C-3).** Decide the fixed allowlist of permissions that stay bound to the immutable `owner` role and can NEVER enter a custom role even if the owner holds them — recommend `tenant:delete`, `tenant:transfer`, `billing:update`, and the role-management (`ac:*`) permissions. Pure creator-subset (BA's default) is not enough; within a tenant the blast radius of a bad custom role is the whole business. This is a product/security policy call, not an implementation detail.

2. **Own the role endpoints, or expose BA's directly?** Recommend: **wrap them in RestOS controllers.** You need the wrapper anyway for the assignment-subset enforcement (C-1), the `ac`-resource reconciliation (C-2), non-delegatable enforcement (C-3), audit of role-definition changes (H-2), cross-tenant pinning (M-3), and rate-limiting (M-4). Exposing BA's `/api/auth/organization/*-role` routes straight to the browser gives you the create-time subset check for free but none of the above. The wrapper is the leverage point — decide to build it and route everything through it.

3. **Is 08.3 the right thing to build right now, before Phase 10 (Admin Order Intake) closes the MVP-1 revenue spine?** The dependency (08.2) is satisfied and the trigger (multi-member tenant) is real, so it's *technically* sequenced correctly — but MVP-1's "can take a customer's money" bar (Phase 10) isn't done. If the first paying restaurant is a single-owner shop, custom roles deliver zero revenue value on day one. Confirm 08.3 is pulled forward on real demand (a specific multi-member tenant), not on "the feature is interesting." If it's speculative, the smallest shippable slice is **C-1+C-2+C-3 hardening with a hardcoded 1–2 role templates**, deferring the full owner role-builder UI.
