<objective>
Ship the central security delta (CTO CRITICAL-3): flip member brand-scope from default-allow to
default-deny (D-08), make `BrandScopeGuard` apply by default with an explicit `@BrandNeutral`
opt-out (D-11, mirroring how `@Public` works for `AuthGuard`), and reconcile every request's
resolved brand against the session pin from Plan 03 (D-10) — a non-owner mismatch OR a non-owner
null pin on a brand-scoped route returns an existence-hiding 404, mirroring the existing
`auth.tenant_mismatch` cross-check. Keep the owner path unrestricted (owner bypass runs BEFORE the
scope lookup) so a freshly-onboarded owner with zero scope rows is never locked out. Because the
guard goes default-on, EVERY controller must be classified and every non-brand-scoped controller
must carry `@BrandNeutral` — produced as a grep-backed audit artifact covering webhooks,
BA-mounted/auth, health, invitation/password-reset/onboarding, and internal-token routes (a missed
brand-neutral route 403s a critical production flow).

Purpose: this is the actual shippable security win of the phase. The default-allow `if (scope
=== null) return true` (brand-scope.guard.ts:77) is a privilege-escalation hole under the new
model; flipping it correctly (role-aware, owner-safe) is the part that needed the security pass.
The default-on flip's blast radius (a forgotten @BrandNeutral 403s webhooks/onboarding/password
reset) is the other half that needs an explicit, complete, grep-verifiable audit — not a generic
"@Public health/auth" hand-wave.

Output: role-aware scope semantics in the reader port + list-my-brands, default-deny + default-on
guard with `@BrandNeutral`, D-10 reconciliation (incl. non-owner null-pin → 404), a complete
controller-classification audit artifact, and the inverted/extended unit test matrix.

Sequencing caution: the flip and the default-on guard land WITH their tests and the
owner-null-scope-unrestricted guarantee in this single plan — never ship a flip untested. Depends
on Plan 03 for `req.activeBrandId` (the pin the reconciliation reads) AND Plan 03's deterministic
initial pin (so a normal non-owner brand-scoped request is non-null-pinned in practice; the
null-pin → 404 here is the defense-in-depth backstop, not the happy path).

Blast-radius caution (default-on): making the guard default-on means EVERY route that does not
carry `@BrandNeutral` runs the guard. The guard throws `brand.operator_required` (L56) for any
non-operator principal once a brand resolves, and `brand.context_required` when no brand is in ALS
— which 403s anonymous customer routes, webhooks, health checks, BA-mounted auth, and every
tenant-level operator route unless they opt out. The mitigation is the COMPLETE `@BrandNeutral`
enumeration backed by the Task 2 audit artifact; Plan 06 adds a positive e2e proving public
traffic survives the flip.

Scope guard (D-01/D-02): brand-level only. `member_brand_scope.role` column left untouched
(skeptic LOW). No `locations`. No `dynamicAccessControl`.
</objective>

## Default-On Blast Radius (D-11)

Flipping `BrandScopeGuard` to default-on means it runs on EVERY route without `@BrandNeutral`. The
guard throws `brand.operator_required` (L56) for any non-operator principal once a brand resolves,
and `brand.context_required` when no brand is in ALS — so the blast radius includes all `@Public`
routes (menu, orders, checkout, signup, brand-onboarding), the Stripe webhook, health/readiness,
internal-token routes, and every tenant-level operator route. The mitigation is the COMPLETE
`@BrandNeutral` enumeration backed by the Task 2 grep-backed audit artifact (every controller
classified, every non-brand-scoped one annotated), unit-covered in Task 3, and e2e-regression-guarded
by Plan 06's positive public-traffic + cross-brand payout cases. The named highest-risk misses the
audit forces coverage of: Stripe webhook, internal provisioning/offboarding, health, and the
