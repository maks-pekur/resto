<objective>
Put the two SPAs where the founder's URL table says they live — `<apex>/admin` and
`<slug>.<apex>/qr` — by deleting the machinery that existed only because they used to have
nowhere to live, and stop the apex itself serving "Restaurant not found".

Purpose: under three apexes the SPA hostnames had no origin, so each SPA carried a
reverse-proxy Worker to reach the API same-origin. Under one apex both hostnames are ordinary
Caddy-served hostnames. The proxies, their cache logic, their `X-Forwarded-Host` handling and
their `cf` options are not rewritten — they are deleted. Roughly 250 lines of Worker source and
440 lines of Worker tests go, and three of the defects the review rounds raised against the
edge-cache subsystem stop existing rather than getting another fix.

Output: two assets-only Worker projects with correct base paths, a QR sticker path that works,
two cart namespaces instead of one shared one, a deliberate landing page on the apex, and a
runbook that describes the system that exists.
</objective>

**The trap, measured under `wrangler 4.129.0 dev --local` (M-09), not read from the docs.**
`not_found_handling: "single-page-application"` serves `/index.html` at the **asset root**, not
the subdirectory's index. With assets nested under `dist/qr/` and nothing at `dist/index.html`,
`/qr/` and `/qr/assets/app.js` are 200 and `/qr/items/abc` is **404** — every deep link dead,
including the sticker path. The fix that was measured working is one extra build step copying
`dist/qr/index.html` to `dist/index.html`; with it, `/qr/t/<token>` returns the shell.

**Billing, and it is why assets-only rather than a Worker script**
(`[CITED: developers.cloudflare.com/workers/static-assets/billing-and-limitations/]`):

> "Requests to static assets are free and unlimited. Requests to the Worker script … are billed
> according to Workers pricing."
>
> "**Important note for free tier users**: When using `run_worker_first`, requests matching the
> specified patterns will always invoke your Worker script. If you exceed your free tier request
> limits, these requests will receive a 429 (Too Many Requests) response instead of falling back
> to static asset serving."
