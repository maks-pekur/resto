## TL;DR

1. **The phase's own goal sentence is not achievable by the phase.** SPEC:9 promises "a dine-in guest scans the QR and the order reaches the kitchen." No guest can place a dine-in order after 10.3 ships, and D-01 says so plainly. This is not a documentation nit — it means the order↔table contract (D-03/D-04/D-06), which is a **breaking change to a published OpenAPI artifact gated by CI**, gets designed with zero real callers and will be designed a second time when the checkout phase arrives. **The sequencing is wrong.** Say it out loud: the cheapest path to real dine-in revenue is the QR-menu checkout on today's free-text `?table=`, and 10.3 is the refinement that follows it.
2. **TBL-09 introduces a correctness regression, not a fix.** The order goes to location B; the stop list the guest ordered from is still location A's, because `GET /v1/menu/availability` resolves the default location and takes no table (`get-menu-availability.service.ts:29`). Its acceptance criterion passes while the kitchen receives orders for items it has 86'd. Nothing in the out-of-scope list mentions this. The fix is cheap (`MENU_AVAILABILITY_S_MAXAGE_SECONDS = 5` — D-13's cache argument does not apply) and must be in scope or TBL-09 must be cut.
3. **D-12 is factually wrong about the code, and its only workaround is a security downgrade.** Admin does **not** have `location:update` (`system-roles.ts:27` — read-only). Printing is owner-only, full stop. And `location:update` also gates archiving _any_ location by UUID on a `@LocationNeutral()` route (`locations.controller.ts:103,153`), so granting a manager the right to print a sticker grants them the right to archive the restaurant.

## Verdict on sequencing (the question asked directly)

**The order is wrong, and I will not soften it.**

D-14's argument is sound as far as it goes: 10.3 and 10.1 both rewrite the location-resolution line at `create-order.service.ts:40`, and _resolve-then-check_ is the only composition that works. That justifies 10.3 before 10.1. It does **not** justify 10.3 before the QR-menu checkout.

What each ordering buys the first paying customer:

| Ship first                 | What a restaurant can do the day it lands                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **10.3 alone**             | Nothing new. Zones and tables exist in a database, an owner can print squares of ink. No guest can order dine-in. `apps/qr-menu/src/api/client.ts` has exactly two calls (`fetchMenu`, `fetchAvailability`) — there is no cart screen, no checkout, no `POST /v1/orders`. |
| **QR-menu checkout alone** | Real dine-in orders, today, using the free-text `?table=` path that already works (`App.tsx:117`) with a hand-written or hand-generated sticker. Ugly and forgeable — but it is revenue.                                                                                  |

The phase is being built in the order that is comfortable for the schema, not the order that is valuable for the customer. Three concrete costs of the current order:

- **The API contract is rewritten twice.** D-03 deletes `table` from `CreateOrderInputSchema`; that field is published at `docs/api/openapi.yaml:3542` and generated into `packages/api-client/src/generated/api.ts`, with a drift gate at `.github/workflows/ci.yml:135`. The replacement shape is being chosen without a single client that exercises it. `packages/cart/src/cart.ts:23` — `mode: 'delivery' | 'pickup' | null` — proves the point: the shared cart cannot even _represent_ dine-in, so the checkout phase will change the contract again.
- **TBL-06/07/08/09 are unfalsifiable by the product.** D-01 concedes this: "provable by API-level e2e tests, **not** by a browser walk-through." The founder's own memory note is explicit that `tsc + vitest` ≠ feature works (`feedback_verify_feature_not_call_shape`). This phase is designed to be verified the way that note forbids.
- **The migration argument is weakest right now and strongest later.** "Add `table_id` before real orders exist" is a real argument — but 10.2 D-12 already establishes a dev-database reset is acceptable while no real tenant data exists. The window does not close until the first customer, which is Q1 2027.

**Recommendation, in order of preference:**

1. **Fold the QR-menu checkout into 10.3** and make it one phase: "dine-in ordering, end to end." Ship it with a browser walk-through as the acceptance test.
2. If that is too large for one phase: keep 10.3 as **schema + admin CRUD + the printed artefact**, and move **TBL-06, TBL-07, TBL-08, TBL-09, D-03, D-04, D-06** into the checkout phase where a client exists to shape them. That is roughly 40% of the phase moved to where it can be verified.
3. Do **not** ship the ordering-contract change with no client. That is the one option with no defenders.

## Is any of this MVP-1?

**The case for cutting or shrinking:**

- MVP-1's gate is _"first paying restaurant takes paid orders end-to-end via web"_ (ROADMAP:7). That gate is already met by the website pickup/delivery flow (Phases 8 + 10). Dine-in via QR is a **second acquisition channel**, not the revenue spine.
- Phase 10's own verification plan is still open (`10-13-PLAN.md`, ROADMAP:698) and 10.1 (pause / opening hours — the thing a launched restaurant hits in week one) is unbuilt. 10.3 was inserted ahead of both.
- Solo founder; ~5 months to the Q1 2027 target. Every phase inserted into MVP-1 has to earn its slot against "the restaurant cannot take money without it." 10.3 does not clear that bar; the checkout does.
- The 90%-value, 5%-cost version already exists: the operator writes `?table=12` into a QR generator on the web and prints it. That is a bad product, but it is a working product, and it costs zero engineering days.

**The case against cutting:**

- A restaurant onboarding for dine-in genuinely needs stickers on opening day. Hand-generating 40 QR codes in a browser tool is not something you can sell.
- `orders.table_id` is a schema decision with a composite FK. Doing it while the database can be reset is materially cheaper than doing it against live orders.
- D-14 is right that 10.3 must precede 10.1.

**My call:** keep the schema and the admin CRUD in MVP-1 — they are the onboarding prerequisite and they are cheap. Move the guest-facing half and the ordering-contract change behind the checkout. Cut or complete TBL-09. Shrink the printing to a print stylesheet.

## Findings

### BLOCK-1 — The phase's stated goal is not deliverable, and the ordering contract is being designed against tests

**Anchors:** SPEC:9 (goal), D-01, `apps/qr-menu/src/api/client.ts` (whole file — two functions), `apps/website/components/checkout/checkout-form.tsx:74`, `packages/cart/src/cart.ts:23`, `docs/api/openapi.yaml:3542`, `.github/workflows/ci.yml:135`

D-01 verified the gap honestly and I confirm all three legs of it:

- `apps/qr-menu/src/api/client.ts` exports `fetchMenu` and `fetchAvailability`. Nothing else. `App.tsx` renders `MenuScreen` with no `cartPrimaryAction`, so the shared `CartSheet` (`packages/ui/src/guest/cart-sheet.tsx:27` — `primaryAction?: ReactNode`) renders a cart with **no checkout button**.
- `checkout-form.tsx:74` — `const resolvedMode = mode === 'delivery' ? 'delivery' : 'pickup';`. `dine_in` is unreachable from the website.
- Therefore the `dine_in` branch of `CreateOrderInputSchema` (`dto.ts:28,45`) has never been exercised by a client.

What D-01 does **not** say, and should: `packages/cart/src/cart.ts:23` types `mode` as `'delivery' | 'pickup' | null`. The shared cart store — used by `apps/website`, `apps/qr-menu` and `packages/ui` alike — cannot represent dine-in at all. Whatever contract 10.3 freezes will meet a widened union in the next phase.

**Why this blocks:** D-03 removes a field from a published API contract. D-04 changes the semantics of a documented validation rule. D-06 rewrites the location-resolution line every order flows through. All three are irreversible-ish decisions made with no consumer to validate them, and CI will force the artifact regeneration whether or not the plan remembers it.

**Fix:** fold in the checkout, or move TBL-06/07/08/09 + D-03/D-04/D-06 to it.

### BLOCK-2 — TBL-09 sends the order to location B while the guest ordered from location A's stop list

**Anchors:** TBL-09, D-06, `apps/api/src/contexts/catalog/application/availability/get-menu-availability.service.ts:29`, `apps/api/src/contexts/catalog/interfaces/http/public-menu.controller.ts:168-171,183`, `apps/api/src/contexts/catalog/domain/menu-cache.ts:3`

`GET /v1/menu/availability` is `@Public() @LocationNeutral()` and its service does this:

```ts
// get-menu-availability.service.ts:29
const locationId = await this.defaultLocation.resolveForTenant(tenantId);
```

It takes no table, no location, no parameter of any kind. It always answers with the **default** location's 86 list — `DefaultLocationResolverService` returns the oldest active location (`default-location-resolver.service.ts:12-18`).

Today that is _consistent but wrong_: the guest sees location A's stop list and the order lands at location A. After TBL-09 it becomes _inconsistent_: the guest at a location-B table sees location A's stop list, orders an item that B has stopped, and the order lands in **B's** feed. B's stop list — the operator's only tool for "we ran out" — has no effect on QR orders at all. The kitchen cannot make the item; the operator cancels and refunds; the guest is angry.

TBL-09's acceptance criterion ("an order from a table in location B is stored with `location_id = B` and appears only in location B's feed") **passes** in that scenario. That is the definition of a test that verifies the mechanism instead of the outcome.

The out-of-scope list (SPEC:100-109) does not mention the menu/availability read path. This is the thing the phase secretly requires and nobody listed.

**The fix is small, and the usual objection does not apply.** "Claude's Discretion" correctly cites 10.1 D-13 — `/v1/menu` carries `s-maxage=300`, so per-guest state would shatter its cache. But `MENU_AVAILABILITY_S_MAXAGE_SECONDS = 5` (`menu-cache.ts:3`). A five-second cache keyed by location is one extra entry per location. There is no cache argument against making availability table-aware.

**Fix (pick one, do not ship neither):**

- (a) Add a location/table input to `GET /v1/menu/availability` and thread the scanned table's location through it. ~20 lines.
- (b) Cut TBL-09 and state explicitly that multi-location QR ordering is unsupported until the availability path is location-aware.

### HIGH-1 — D-12's stated consequence is false, and the only workaround grants "archive any location"

**Anchors:** D-12, `packages/domain/src/rbac/system-roles.ts:13,27,32`, `apps/api/src/contexts/tenancy/interfaces/http/locations.controller.ts:103,138,153`, `apps/api/src/contexts/identity/interfaces/http/guards/location-scope.guard.ts:29-34`, `packages/domain/src/rbac/permissions.ts:10`

D-12 says: _"a manager cannot reprint a damaged sticker without an owner or admin."_

The code says otherwise:

```
system-roles.ts:13   owner:  location: ['read', 'create', 'update', 'delete']
system-roles.ts:27   admin:  location: ['read']
system-roles.ts:32   staff:  location: ['read']
```

**Only the owner has `location:update`.** An admin cannot print, cannot create a zone, cannot add a table, cannot rename anything on this surface. The entire floor-setup workflow — the thing a general manager does during onboarding, not the investor-owner — is owner-only. The founder accepted a risk that is one notch worse than the one that was described to them.

Worse, the escape hatch is dangerous. An owner can mint a custom role granting `location:update` (subset-of-actor check at `effective-permissions.ts:40-52` allows it). But `location:update` also gates:

```
locations.controller.ts:138   @Patch(':id')          @Permissions({ location: ['update'] })
locations.controller.ts:153   @Patch(':id/archive')  @Permissions({ location: ['update'] })
```

…on a controller marked `@LocationNeutral()` (`:103`). `LocationScopeGuard` returns `true` immediately for location-neutral routes (`location-scope.guard.ts:29-32`) and otherwise keys **only** on the ALS-bound location, never on a route parameter (`:34`). So a member holding that custom role can `PATCH` or **archive any location in the tenant by UUID**, ignoring `member_location_scope` entirely.

Net: to let a shift manager reprint a sticker you must hand them the ability to archive the restaurant's other venue.

**Friday-night answer to the question asked:** the sticker is destroyed, the owner is on a plane. Nobody can reprint. And because there is no operator order-entry path (HIGH-2) and the free-text `?table=` fallback is deleted by TBL-06, **that table cannot place an order for the rest of the night.** In a 20-table room that is 5% of covers, silently.

**Fix:** add one action. `location: ['read','create','update','delete','tables']`, or a small `table: ['read','manage']` resource, in `permissions.ts:10` + `system-roles.ts` (admin and staff get read; admin gets manage). This is exactly what Phase 10 already did for `order: ['cancel']` (ROADMAP:664 — "RBAC: `order:cancel` verb, preset re-sync for existing tenants"), so the pattern and the migration cost are both known. D-12's "no new permission resource is introduced" is not simplicity — it is a permission-granularity bug wearing simplicity's coat.

### HIGH-2 — There is no fallback when the QR path fails, and no phase in any milestone adds one

**Anchors:** `apps/api/src/contexts/ordering/interfaces/http/operator-orders.controller.ts:56,87,98,121`, TBL-06, ROADMAP (grepped MVP-1/2/3 + `.planning/seeds/`)

`OperatorOrdersController` exposes exactly four routes: `GET feed`, `GET :id/detail`, `POST :id/accept`, `POST :id/advance`. **There is no operator order creation anywhere in the API**, and grepping ROADMAP.md and `.planning/seeds/` for operator/manual/counter order entry returns nothing across MVP-1, MVP-2 and MVP-3.

TBL-06 then removes the last manual affordance: _"the free-text `?table=` path is removed."_ PR #275 had already removed the manual table-number input.

So after 10.3 + the checkout phase, the **only** way an order can enter RestOS is a guest scanning a working sticker with a working phone with working data. Every one of these has no recovery:

- sticker destroyed, defaced, or peeled off (and only the owner can reprint — HIGH-1)
- guest's phone battery is dead, or the camera app cannot read QR
- no mobile data and the restaurant Wi-Fi is captive-portalled or saturated (see MEDIUM-4)
- the party moved tables after being seated
- a guest who simply does not want to use their phone to order

In every case, staff can do nothing. They cannot key the order in. This is the single largest day-one gap in the phase's blast radius, and it is in nobody's scope.

**Fix (minimum viable):** either an operator "create an order for table X" path, or — cheaper — a staff-side screen that shows any table's QR on the operator's own phone so it can be held out to the guest. The second is nearly free once TBL-10 exists, and it also solves HIGH-1's Friday night: the manager shows the code on a tablet instead of reprinting it. It needs `location:read`, not `location:update`.

### HIGH-3 — Freezing a formatted label string locks the order's language; store zone name + number instead

**Anchors:** TBL-08, SPEC:163 (`Зал 1 · стол 14`), `apps/qr-menu/src/i18n/en.json:35`, `apps/qr-menu/src/i18n/ru.json:35`, `apps/admin/src/lib/i18n/messages/{en,es,ru}.json`

The existing guest code already does this correctly:

```json
en.json:35   "table.current": "Table {table}"
ru.json:35   "table.current": "Стол {table}"
```

The noun comes from the **client's** locale; only the value is interpolated. `TableBanner.tsx` renders `t('table.current', { table })`.

TBL-08 replaces that with a server-frozen string containing the Russian words "Зал" and "стол". The resolution endpoint has no locale input. Admin ships **three** locale files (en/es/ru). Consequences:

- An English-speaking tourist scanning a table sees `Зал 1 · стол 14`.
- A Spanish-speaking operator's order card shows `Зал 1 · стол 14`.
- Every future surface (guest status page, receipt, kitchen ticket, exports) inherits the lock.

The correct design is **less** work, not more: snapshot `table_zone_name` (operator-authored, legitimately as-typed) and `table_number` as two columns. Each surface formats with its own `t()`. Same immutability guarantee against renames. Bonus: a later "group the feed by zone" needs no string parsing.

This is a schema decision. Reversing it after orders exist means parsing `" · "` out of historical rows.

### HIGH-4 — D-08's mitigation does not survive the scissors, and the sheet as dimensioned may not scan after cutting

**Anchors:** D-08, D-09, TBL-10

Two independent physical problems. Neither is re-litigating the founder's aesthetic decision; both are printing requirements.

**(a) Deterministic ordering is destroyed by the first physical operation.**

D-08's required mitigation is "tables ascending, left-to-right then top-to-bottom, one zone per document." That information lives in the _sheet layout_. A 20-table zone is four sheets (D-09). The first thing a staff member does is cut out 24 squares. The moment the second square leaves the paper, the ordering is gone — 24 visually identical squares on a counter, in whatever order they fell.

The recovery path exists but is expensive and error-prone: scan each square with a phone, read the label TBL-06 renders, stick it, repeat 24 times. One mis-scan or one interruption and a sticker is on the wrong table — invisible until a guest's order lands in the wrong section. That failure has no detection mechanism anywhere in the phase.

**(b) The 6-per-A4 geometry may not leave a valid quiet zone.**

A4 = 210 × 297 mm. A 2×3 grid gives 105 × 99 mm cells. A QR symbol requires a 4-module quiet zone on all sides. The URL `https://<slug>.menu.<domain>/?t=<uuid>` is ~65–70 bytes → roughly a 33×33-module symbol at level M. If the _code area_ is 90 mm, one module is ~2.7 mm and the quiet zone is ~11 mm per side, so the code needs ~112 mm of white — **wider than the 105 mm column**. Either the "8–9 cm" figure means the whole drawn square including quiet zone (in which case say so, and the code area is ~73 mm), or the sheet is specified to print codes that cannot carry a legal margin.

Then compound it with (a): bare codes, no crop marks, staff cutting by eye. They will cut into the quiet zone. Scanning degrades in exactly the conditions D-09 optimises for — dim restaurant light, an arm's length away, a cheap phone camera.

**Fix — costs no design work, puts nothing on the sticker:**

1. State whether 8–9 cm includes the quiet zone. Draw the quiet zone as part of the image.
2. Print a hairline cut guide _outside_ the quiet zone, so scissors have a target that is not the code.
3. Print the table number **in the gutter, outside the cut line**, so it lands on the offcut. This fully solves (a) and never appears on the finished sticker. If the founder rejects even this, then the download screen must instruct: _"cut and stick one sheet at a time"_ — that is the only procedure under which the deterministic ordering actually holds, and it needs to be said in the UI, not assumed.

### HIGH-5 — D-07 chose a PDF library over a print stylesheet on a rationale the alternative already satisfies

**Anchors:** D-07, `apps/admin/package.json:15-42`, D-09, D-10

D-07 rejects a browser print view because _"the file can be saved and sent to a print shop."_ But a print view **is** a savable file: `@page { size: A4; margin: 0 }` plus a 2×3 CSS grid, then the browser's own "Destination → Save as PDF". That produces a real, vector, A4 PDF with no new dependency, and the SVG QR codes stay vector all the way to the print shop.

What the chosen path costs instead:

- Two new dependencies in `apps/admin` (currently zero PDF and zero QR deps — `package.json:15-42`).
- A raster trap. The default recipe everyone reaches for is `qrcode` → `<canvas>` → `toDataURL('image/png')` → `jsPDF.addImage`. At 300 dpi a 90 mm code is ~1060 px; a 50-table zone is ~9 sheets of large base64 PNGs assembled in a browser tab. It will be slow on the laptop a restaurant owner actually has, and the output will be raster in a document whose only purpose is printing.
- If the library stays, the plan **must** state vector output explicitly — draw modules as `rect`s, or feed `qrcode`'s SVG string through `svg2pdf.js`. Left unstated, the implementer ships raster and nobody notices until a print shop complains.

D-07 invites the objection: _"what does the server know that the browser does not — the answer today is nothing."_ Agreed, and it is the wrong question. The right one is _"what does a PDF library know that the browser's own PDF writer does not"_ — and the answer is also nothing.

**Fix:** print stylesheet for the sheet; keep D-10's single-table SVG download (that one is genuinely a few lines and genuinely useful for the future sticker templates). Cut the PDF library.

### MEDIUM-1 — The resolution endpoint must re-verify the host, and should not return `locationId`

**Anchors:** "Claude's Discretion" bullets 1–2, `apps/api/src/contexts/catalog/interfaces/http/public-menu.controller.ts:155-171,255-260`, D-06

Two things:

- **Host scoping.** `PublicMenuController` deliberately does **not** trust the ALS-bound tenant; every handler calls `requireGuestTenantOr404` (`:255-260`) to re-verify the request's own host against `resolveByCustomerHost`, because the middleware's fallback chain resolves a broader set of hosts (`:158-166`). The table-resolution endpoint is a sibling public route and must do exactly the same, or tenant A's table ids resolve — and leak zone names — on any host that reaches the API.
- **Drop `locationId` from the response.** The discretion block justifies it as _"so the guest app can ... later submit an order without a second round trip."_ But per D-03/D-06 the guest submits only the table id and the **server** derives the location (`create-order.service.ts:40`). Nothing consumes it. It is a gratuitous internal identifier on an unauthenticated, publicly cacheable response. Either delete it, or give it a job by fixing availability (BLOCK-2) — in which case it earns its place.

Also note: a long public cache on this route means an archived table keeps resolving, and a renamed zone keeps returning the old name, for the cache lifetime. Pick a TTL that matches how fast an operator expects an archive to take effect and write the number down.

### MEDIUM-2 — Resolve the table _after_ the idempotency short-circuit

**Anchors:** `apps/api/src/contexts/ordering/application/create-order.service.ts:40,42-45`, D-06

Current order of operations:

```ts
40   const locationId = await this.defaultLocation.resolveForTenant(tenantId);
42   const existingOrder = await this.repo.findByIdempotencyKey(tenantId, input.idempotencyKey);
43   if (existingOrder) return toOrderResponse(existingOrder.toSnapshot());
```

D-06 replaces line 40 with a table resolution that **throws** on an unresolvable table (TBL-07). If it stays at line 40, a retry of an already-successful order stops being idempotent the moment the table is archived — the client gets a 4xx instead of the existing order. That is a real window during service: an operator archives a table while a guest's payment retry is in flight.

The location is not needed by the idempotency check. Move the table resolution below line 45. One-line note in the plan; cheap to get right, annoying to debug later.

(Minor: CONTEXT D-06 and SPEC:19 cite `create-order.service.ts:41`; the call is at :40.)

### MEDIUM-3 — Public order creation burns the location's daily short-number sequence before any payment

**Anchors:** `apps/api/src/contexts/ordering/interfaces/http/orders.controller.ts:36,45-54`, `create-order.service.ts:126-132`, `apps/api/src/shared/security.ts:66-74`, `apps/api/src/config/env.schema.ts:255`

`POST /v1/orders` is `@Public()` and unauthenticated. `nextShortNumber` is called at **creation** (`:126`), before the payment intent exists. Anonymous requests key the rate limiter on `ip:${req.ip}` (`security.ts:73`) with `RATE_LIMIT_PUBLIC_PER_MIN` defaulting to 60 (`env.schema.ts:255`).

So anyone can create 60 unpaid orders per minute and drive the location's daily short number — the number staff shout across the kitchen — to four digits inside an hour. The orders never reach the feed (the feed counts `status === 'paid'`), so nobody sees the abuse until the numbering looks absurd.

This is pre-existing, not caused by 10.3. But 10.3 is the phase that prints the entry point on the furniture and puts it in a window facing the street. Worth a line in the phase's risk list even if the fix lands elsewhere: gate `nextShortNumber` on payment, not on creation.

### MEDIUM-4 — Every guest on the restaurant's own Wi-Fi shares one rate-limit bucket

**Anchors:** `apps/api/src/shared/security.ts:66-74`, `apps/api/src/config/env.schema.ts:255`, `apps/qr-menu/src/App.tsx:22,68-91`, ROADMAP:646

Anonymous guests have no session cookie, so `rateLimitKeyGenerator` falls through to `ip:${req.ip}` (`security.ts:73`). Guests on the house Wi-Fi all present one NAT address.

`App.tsx:22` sets `AVAILABILITY_POLL_MS = 20_000` and the poller runs while the tab is visible — 3 req/min per open menu, plus the periodic menu refetch, plus (new in 10.3) a table-resolution call per scan. At `RATE_LIMIT_PUBLIC_PER_MIN = 60`, roughly **15–18 concurrent guests** exhaust the entire restaurant's public budget and the next guest to scan gets a 429 instead of a menu.

Phase 10 identified this exact NAT problem — _"The shared 60-req/min per-IP rate limit must be fixed for polling to work from several devices behind one restaurant NAT"_ (ROADMAP:646) — and it was resolved for **operators** only, because an authenticated request keys on `userId` (`security.ts:67`). Guests were never covered.

10.3 is precisely the phase that makes "twenty guests on one Wi-Fi, all with the menu open" the normal Friday-evening case that `CLAUDE.md` §Performance names as the peak-load assumption. Either raise the guest budget, or key guests on something finer than IP (a first-party cookie set on first load), or drop the availability poll to an event-driven refresh.

### MEDIUM-5 — The table `number` type is unpinned, and `1..N` is not how restaurants number tables

**Anchors:** TBL-02, TBL-03

TBL-02 calls `number` "a display label only." TBL-03 auto-numbers `1..N`. The type is never stated, and the acceptance criteria only ever use integers.

Real floors: `A1`–`A8` on the terrace, `T12`, `бар-3`, `101`–`112` upstairs, `VIP`. Every restaurant I have seen has at least one non-numeric label. If `number` is an `integer` column the model is wrong on the first onboarding call. If it is `text`, say so, and say what the uniqueness index compares and how the list sorts (`ORDER BY number` on text puts `10` before `2` — which breaks TBL-10's "ascending by table number" ordering, the very thing D-08 depends on).

Also: bulk creation should take a **start offset**, not just a count. "Terrace, tables 21–40" is one call; without an offset it is one call plus 20 renames. That is a two-field change now versus a rework later.

### MEDIUM-9 — "An order is paid up front and closed" assumes away how sit-down dine-in actually works

**Anchors:** SPEC:103-104, D-01 (deferred checkout phase), Phase 8 payment model

SPEC:103 excludes occupancy and open tabs _"because there is no concept of an open bill; an order is paid up front and closed."_ SPEC:104 excludes moving and merging for the same reason.

That is a coherent engineering position and a large, unexamined product bet. Sit-down dine-in is multi-round: drinks, then food, then another round, then dessert. Under a pay-up-front model that is **three or four separate orders and three or four separate card transactions per table.** Card processing carries a fixed per-transaction component; on a €6 beer that fixed component is a meaningfully larger share of the ticket than on a €40 basket. Four rounds pay it four times.

It also produces four entries in the operator's feed for one table with no way to see they belong together — no occupancy, no open tab, no merge, and (SPEC:107) no zone filter.

This model works for fast-casual counter service. It is the standard failure mode for QR ordering in a sit-down restaurant. Nothing in MVP-1, MVP-2 or MVP-3 revisits it. That is fine as a **stated** bet; it is not fine as an unstated one buried in an out-of-scope justification. Write it down as an assumption with a revisit trigger (e.g. "first tenant that is not counter-service"), because if it is wrong, no amount of QR polish saves the phase.

## Under-engineering: what a real restaurant needs on day one that is in nobody's scope

1. **A way to take an order when the QR path fails** (HIGH-2). Not in this phase, not in MVP-1, not in MVP-2, not in MVP-3.
2. **A print permission that is not "archive the restaurant"** (HIGH-1).
3. **A location-correct stop list for the location the guest is actually sitting in** (BLOCK-2).
4. **Non-numeric table labels** (MEDIUM-5). Every floor has at least one.
5. **Any signal that a table's sticker was mis-stuck** (HIGH-4a). No detection, no reconciliation, no "this table has had no orders all week" flag.
6. **A guest rate-limit model that survives a full restaurant on one Wi-Fi** (MEDIUM-4).
7. **A stated position on multi-round dine-in** (MEDIUM-9).

## What I would cut

1. **The client-side PDF library.** Replace with `@page`/CSS-grid print view + the browser's own Save-as-PDF. Keep the single-table SVG download. _(HIGH-5)_
2. **TBL-09 as specified** — either cut it outright and state that multi-location QR ordering is unsupported, or expand scope to make `/v1/menu/availability` location-aware. Shipping it alone is a net regression. _(BLOCK-2)_
3. **The `locationId` field on the public resolution response.** No consumer. _(MEDIUM-1)_
4. **The `?location=all` picker (D-13).** The router cannot reach the state. _(MEDIUM-8)_
5. **The formatted `table_label` column** — replaced, not deleted, by `table_zone_name` + `table_number`. _(HIGH-3)_
6. **Roughly 40% of the phase, by resequencing:** TBL-06, TBL-07, TBL-08, TBL-09 and D-03/D-04/D-06 move into the QR-menu checkout phase, where a real client can shape and verify them. What stays in 10.3: TBL-01, TBL-02, TBL-03, TBL-04, TBL-05, TBL-10, TBL-11 — schema, CRUD, scoping, printing, admin screen. Every one of those is verifiable on its own and is a genuine onboarding prerequisite.

## What should NOT be deferred

1. **The QR-menu checkout.** It is the only thing here that produces revenue, and it should lead rather than follow. Every day 10.3 ships before it is a day of unexercised contract design.
2. **A print/reprint permission distinct from `location:update`.** One line in `permissions.ts`, and the alternative is either an owner-only bottleneck on Friday night or a role that can archive locations.
3. **The availability location fix, if TBL-09 ships.** A five-second cache is not a cache-shattering problem; a kitchen receiving orders it has 86'd is a refund and a bad review.
