# The 08.4-07 follow-up is closed

`08.4-07-SUMMARY.md` flagged one thing as "the single most important thing a reviewer of this plan
should know": `LocationPermissionChecker` was built, unit-proven and **deliberately not wired** as
the live `PERMISSION_CHECKER`, because `PermissionsGuard` never passed `activeLocationId` and
swapping the binding without that would have locked out every non-owner. That judgement was right.

It is now wired, and the plumbing it was waiting for exists.

## What it cost while it waited

Found while preparing the Phase 10 walkthrough, not by a test. `manager@demo.local` — correctly
seeded as `staff` at tenant level with the `manager` preset at two pizza locations — was denied
**everything**: order feed, order detail, cancel, refund. The live checker was
`BetterAuthPermissionChecker`, which evaluates BA's tenant-wide member role and, in its own words,
"has zero location concept". So every non-owner got only their system role, and `staff` grants
`{ tenant: read, location: read }` — nothing operational.

In other words the location-scoped access this phase delivered was inert: managers, cashiers and
kitchen staff could sign in and do nothing. Phase 10's walkthrough step 10 ("sign in as a non-owner
and confirm they can cancel but see no refund section") could never have passed.

## Why the port hid it

`PermissionChecker` declared three parameters. `LocationPermissionChecker.hasPermission` took a
fourth, `activeLocationId`. TypeScript permits an implementation to add optional parameters no
caller supplies, so this compiled, passed its own unit tests — which called the class directly with
all four — and denied every request in production. The parameter is now on the **port**, where a
missing argument is a compile error.

## The design change the wiring forced

A location-only checker deadlocks. Picking a location goes through
`POST /v1/me/set-active-location`, which is itself gated on `tenant: ['read']`, so a member without
a location could never acquire one. Swapping the binding alone reproduced exactly that: `403` on the
very call that would have fixed it.

Effective permissions are now the **union** of the tenant system role and the role held at the
active location — `staff` supplies the baseline that bootstraps a session, `member_location_scope`
supplies what the member may do there (D-06). This is strictly additive against the previous live
behaviour: everything the BA checker allowed is still allowed, and the location role adds to it.
Owners still short-circuit before any lookup.

## Verified

Against the running API, both accounts pinned to Kyiv Center:

| account              | set-active-location | order feed | order detail | catalog | refund  |
| -------------------- | ------------------- | ---------- | ------------ | ------- | ------- |
| `owner@demo.local`   | 200                 | 200        | 200          | 200     | **200** |
| `manager@demo.local` | 200                 | 200        | 200          | 200     | **403** |

The manager row is Phase 10 step 10's contract exactly: they can work the order, and refunding is
closed to them because `billing` belongs to the owner preset alone.

Unit coverage rewritten rather than re-pointed. The two specs that failed had encoded the deadlock as
correct ("returns false for a non-owner with no activeLocationId"); they now assert the union, that
the union is not a blank cheque, that the bootstrap call cannot deadlock, and — in the guard spec —
that `activeLocationId` actually reaches the checker, which is the assertion whose absence let this
survive.
